# Why it works

Learning to Fly is a reading game for autistic children, built first for one learner. This page links each feature to the research behind it. Where a choice is ours rather than a study's, it says so.

One finding shapes everything else: the evidence on teaching reading comprehension to autistic learners is thin. Most studies are small, and few meet the What Works Clearinghouse standards ([Singh et al., 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40489-020-00200-3); [Zhang et al., 2023](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1361852)). So the app measures its own effect for each child, through its baseline, its reviews, and the reports in the grown-ups' area, before anyone claims it works.

## Starting in the right place

- **Scouting places him by reading profile, not by age or diagnosis.** Across 36 studies, autistic readers averaged below their peers in comprehension, with word-meaning knowledge and decoding the strongest predictors, and individual scores ran from floor to ceiling. Scouting checks word reading, reading comprehension and listening comprehension separately, and switches on read-aloud when it helps. [Brown et al., 2013](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-012-1638-1); [Nation et al., 2006](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ748110)
- **Texts start low in social load.** Texts that depend on social knowledge are harder for autistic readers, so every text is tagged for social load, and feelings and intentions are taught on purpose. [Brown et al., 2013](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-012-1638-1)

## Teaching each skill

- **Watch, Together, Your turn.** Direct, explicit instruction is the most-used method in the intervention studies and is rated highly effective. Every skill is shown, then practised with help, then done alone. [Tárraga-Mínguez et al., 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/11/1/3); [Finnegan & Mazin, 2016](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/798031f1-cac6-4ad5-97c5-d6d1c53b12e5); [Steinbrenner et al., 2020](https://ncaep.fpg.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/EBP-Report-2020.pdf)
- **Demos drop out once he has shown a skill.** Our choice. It follows the same model (show, then fade the help), applied to a learner who keeps a skill once he has seen how. A right scouting answer or a solved item counts as shown, and every demo has a "Got it, skip" button.
- **Hints wait, then step up.** Prompting, time delay, modelling and reinforcement are established practices, so hints wait a few seconds and step from light to full. [Steinbrenner et al., 2020](https://ncaep.fpg.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/EBP-Report-2020.pdf); [Browder et al., 2006](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001440290607200401)
- **Functional words come with their meaning and a new setting.** Word-reading studies taught warning-label words with their meaning, but transfer to new settings was incomplete, so every skill has items in a new format or setting, and mastery needs one. [Spector, 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ939843); [Dogoe et al., 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ942469)
- **Answers are story maps, sequence strips and sorting.** Visual supports work best: graphic organizers and visually cued instruction scored highest in reviews, and story maps improved quiz scores and retells in small studies. [Finnegan & Mazin, 2016](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/798031f1-cac6-4ad5-97c5-d6d1c53b12e5); [Singh et al., 2021](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/37f47507-2143-410d-8e6c-13070edfb664); [Stringfield et al., 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ947195); [Spencer & Kirby, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/15/8/1020)
- **Who "she" means, and asking your own questions.** Cueing what a pronoun refers to was the only one of three aids with a significant effect for 20 autistic adolescents, so a whole step practises it. [O'Connor & Klein, 2004](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:JADD.0000022603.44077.6b); [Whalon & Hanline, 2008](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ804651)

## Feelings and the thinking strand

- **Understanding is checked by choosing before explaining.** Inference about inner states is the hard part for older autistic readers: retelling was a relative strength while inference fell far below average. [McIntyre et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/2396941520968028); [Tirado & Saldaña, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-015-2648-6)
- **Understanding and expressing are separate, and expressing is never required to move on.** Putting feelings into words is hard for many autistic people, and taught emotion skills often did not last or carry over. [Kinnaird et al., 2019](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/publications/publication_modal/2344580); [Fletcher-Watson et al., 2014](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD008785_review-evidence-use-interventions-people-autism-spectrum-disorder-based-psychological-model-theory)
- **Lantern Peaks opens only after the basics, and is treated as an experiment.** Trials of classroom philosophy are mixed: one estimated about two months' extra reading progress, a larger one found no effect. [EEF, 2015](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/philosophy-for-children); [EEF, 2021](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/philosophy-for-children-effectiveness-trial); [Cassidy et al., 2013](https://pureportal.strath.ac.uk/en/publications/i-find-it-easier-to-get-on-with-people-when-i-use-philosophy-phil/)

## Telling about today

- **"Tell about today" with tiles, the school timetable and specific questions, faded slowly.** Autistic young people recalled fewer specific personal memories and needed more prompts; retelling model stories and then their own, with visuals faded out, improved personal narratives, though gains held unevenly. [Goddard et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954579413000904); [Petersen et al., 2014](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2013_LSHSS-12-0099); [Gauvreau, 2015](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/handle/1773/33741)

## Interests

- **Texts on his interests, and a check on whether they help him.** One child's answers and retell improved when his special interest was put into the text; later studies were mixed. So one pick in five ignores his interests on purpose, and the grown-ups' area compares the two. [El Zein et al., 2016](https://meadowscenter.org/library/embedding-perseverative-interest-of-a-child-with-autism-in-text-may-result-in-improved-reading-comprehension-a-pilot-study/); [Solis et al., 2022](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED634621)

## Rewards and the journey

- **The island, the journey and the flight kit reward effort, never scores.** Expected tangible rewards for performance reduce later free-choice interest, while positive feedback raises it; gamification has small positive effects, and a game story helps behaviour. So praise names the skill, the journey moves with finished sessions, flight points come from every answer whether right or not, and there are no leaderboards. [Deci et al., 1999](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/a-meta-analytic-review-of-experiments-examining-the-effects-of-ex/); [Sailer & Homner, 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1245270)
- **Setbacks on the journey, with a coping line and a choice of plan.** Our choice, to practise staying calm when plans change. No study tested it.
- **The push-past bonus, and the gentle nudge after a pause.** Our choices. The nudge offers read-aloud or a hint, which follows the prompting practice above.

## A grown-up in the loop

- **Talk prompts, a dashboard, a printable report, and about 25 sessions before judging.** Software helps only as a carrier of good teaching: one web program raised comprehension over 26 one-to-one sessions but not in school groups or a short online version, and the pooled effect of group studies is small. A parent-led shared-reading program improved story comprehension. [Bailey et al., 2017](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/effects-of-abracadabra-literacy-instruction-on-children-with-auti/); [Arciuli & Bailey, 2019](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/efficacy-of-abracadabra-literacy-instruction-in-a-school-setting-/); [Bailey et al., 2022](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/a-trial-of-online-abracadabra-literacy-instruction-with-supplemen/); [Zhang et al., 2023](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1361852); [Grindle et al., 2020](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/teaching-children-with-autism-reading-comprehension-skills-using-/); [Lo & Shum, 2021](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/287137)
- **The Activity tab: where sessions end, where he pauses, which sections take longer.** It follows from the thin evidence above: the program watches its own effect for him. The patterns it names are rules of thumb, and each one says what it is based on.

## Calm, predictable design

- **Fixed layout, comfort settings, and asking him what he thinks.** Design guidance for autistic children's software centres on adapting to the individual, controlling sensory stimulation and keeping the interface predictable, and autistic children can take part in design critique. [Davis et al., 2010](https://researchprofiles.herts.ac.uk/en/publications/guidelines-for-researchers-and-practitioners-designing-software-a/); [Sanmartin et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.5753/rbie.2026.7245); [Frauenberger et al., 2013](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Conversing_through_and_about_technologies_design_critique_as_an_opportunity_to_engage_children_with_autism_and_broaden_research_er_perspectives/23394875)

## AI-drafted content

- **Every text passes a reading-level check and a person's review; no AI decides mastery.** A large language model scored children's short reading answers near expert agreement in one study, but a 2026 meta-analysis found such scoring weak overall, and models asked to simplify text struggled to hit a 4th-grade level. [Henkel et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18373); [Hardy, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04820); [Day et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09158)

## Our own choices, to check against his results

These are design decisions, not tested rules. The reports in the grown-ups' area are how we check them.

- **Mastery:** 80% right on his own across two sessions on different days, with one item in a new format or setting.
- **Reviews:** at about 3, 7 and 21 days, back to practice after a missed review.
- **Text levels:** two right in a row moves up a level, two misses step down; levels 4 and 5 open only when he climbs to them.
- **Fast track:** one flawless session with a new format and a level 3 or harder text can master a skill; reviews still check it.
- **The school-goal report:** current classroom goals (main idea, key details, problem-solving), reported on their own so they can be shared with school.
- **Flight point values and gear thresholds.**
